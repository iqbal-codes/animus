# Issue 56: research conclusion

## Conclusion

**The requested one-final-PR spec delivery is not demonstrated by the pinned integration.** Archon supplies potentially useful execution, review, runtime and merge primitives; Factory currently launches the loop rather than a proven exact-child coordinator. Neither source inspection nor the offline probes establish safe parent-graph coordination or an atomic, independently evidenced final-candidate gate. The research supports a design direction and specific proof obligations, not a claim of supported end-to-end operation. [Issue #56](https://github.com/iqbal-codes/labq-software-factory/issues/56) remains open as observed when this note was written.

The issue comments record these **user decisions**, which are requirements, not demonstrated capabilities or authorization to implement or run:

- A spec with subtickets produces exactly **one final PR for the spec**, not one PR per subticket. A standalone ticket produces one PR for that ticket.
- Factory owns selection and claims of admitted subtickets, explicit target binding and serialized integration; Archon executes targeted work and applicable review/verification. The exact-target seam must fail closed until proven.
- Subticket checks are proportionate to the contract; a black-box application check is not required on each isolated subticket branch. The integrated spec candidate gets the black-box application check in the final PR process. A standalone ticket's final candidate gets the applicable check.
- PR creation need not wait solely for that check. Readiness and merge must hold without current independent final-candidate evidence. Evidence must bind to the actual candidate and relevant base/merge tree and be invalidated on material drift. These decisions do not authorize approval, merge or deployment.

Source: [issue and comments](https://github.com/iqbal-codes/labq-software-factory/issues/56). They do not decide all admission and review policy, nor waive isolation, applicable security checks or integration correctness.

## Decisions and implementation proofs still needed

The following are **proposed proof obligations**, not claims about existing support:

- **Graph and authority:** decide the authoritative versioned spec, membership snapshot, admission and dependency semantics (including open siblings), claim ownership, lease/recovery and drift handling. Prove competing coordinators cannot select or integrate the same child incorrectly and that failures remain recoverable without duplicate delivery.
- **Targeted execution:** select a supported Factory-to-Archon interface and validate actual configured source capture, workflow identity, inputs and providers. Prove exact child/contract binding, control operator-message conflicts, fence unauthorized tracker/PR/push capabilities, and fail closed on ambiguity. A prompt or successful launch is not an enforcement boundary.
- **Checkout and integration:** decide branch/worktree lifecycle and exclusive-writer mechanism. Prove expected repository, branch and starting SHA before the first node, ownership throughout execution, and serialized compare-and-swap integration with conflict and restart handling. Baseline records alone are not that barrier.
- **Outcome and scope:** establish trustworthy run identity and inspect terminal state **and** authored outcome, changes and proportionate child checks; acceptance, exit status or `completed` alone is not sufficient. Demonstrate enforcement of no subticket PR and the one-final-PR rule independently of instructions.
- **Final evidence:** define trusted parent-wide review independence and coverage, black-box environment ownership, protected evidence provenance and policy. Bind spec, PR, head H, base B, candidate tree M, runtime snapshot/setup and actual check producer/commit to the same candidate or a proven equivalent. Demonstrate stale-evidence invalidation and revalidation after relevant changes. Preserve private scenario confidentiality; no holdout contents are needed for this research conclusion.
- **Landing:** choose and verify an enforceable final-tree guard, including actual repository rules, bypass actors, merge method and batching behavior. Show the tested/reviewed candidate is the tree permitted to land, not merely that a head check passed. Unknown protection or an unproven queue guarantee must hold merge readiness.

No workflow or evaluator was executed in this research task, no holdout contents were accessed, and no product, tracker or configuration changes were made. Prior probes and repository observations above are cited as prior findings, not newly reproduced here. Implementation, workflow execution and merge require separate authorization.
